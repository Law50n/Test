# Research brief: The Somerton Man / Tamam Shud Case (1948)

Case check: yes, comfortably. A rich, well-documented timeline, a real
physical clue chain (the code, the book, the phone number), and a
genuine, fresh 2022 partial resolution (identity solved, cause of death
still isn't) -- the same "science recently cracked part of it, not all
of it" arc that worked for Dyatlov. Minimal violence in the actual
record (no wounds, no signs of struggle), fits the calm tone well.

## Established facts

- On the morning of 01/12/1948, an unidentified man was found dead,
  propped against a seawall on Somerton Beach, near Adelaide, South
  Australia; he is believed to have died the previous evening.
  [Wikipedia: Somerton Man; Smithsonian Magazine]
- He carried no identification. Every label had been removed from his
  clothing. [Wikipedia; ABC News]
- Fingerprints and dental records were circulated internationally for
  decades with no match. [Wikipedia]
- A pathologist later found a small, hidden fob pocket sewn into his
  trousers containing a tightly rolled scrap of paper printed "Tamam
  Shud" -- Persian for "it is ended" or "finished," the final words of
  the poem collection The Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam, torn from the last
  page of a copy of the book. [Wikipedia; Smithsonian Magazine]
- Weeks later, a man came forward with a copy of that exact rare
  edition of the Rubaiyat, missing its final page, which he said had
  been tossed into the back seat of his unlocked car parked near the
  beach around the time of the death. [Wikipedia]
- Inside the book's back cover: a handwritten code, five lines of
  capital letters (with one line crossed out), and a phone number.
  Despite decades of attempts by both amateur and professional
  codebreakers, the code has never been conclusively deciphered.
  [Mysterious Writings; Wikipedia]
- The phone number led police to a nurse, Jessica "Jestyn" Thomson
  (nee Harkness), who at the time lived roughly 400 meters from where
  the body was found. Questioned in 1949, she denied knowing the man.
  [Wikipedia; ABC News: "six clues"]
- Independently of the death, Jestyn had given a copy of the same rare
  Rubaiyat edition to a man named Alfred "Alf" Boxall in 1945, inscribed
  in her own hand with her phone number. When Boxall turned up alive
  and well in 1949 -- dashing early hope that he might be the dead
  man -- he still had that copy, confirming the phone-number link
  existed independently of the Somerton Man's death itself.
  [tamamshud.blogspot; Wikipedia]
- Decades later, Jestyn's daughter, Kate Thomson (also known as Rachel
  Egan), stated publicly that her mother had privately hinted at
  knowing the dead man's identity but always refused to explain, and
  suggested her mother had lied to police at the time. Jestyn herself
  died in 2007 without ever confirming this. [ABC News; multiple case
  retrospectives]
- Autopsy and toxicology found no external injuries and no signs of a
  struggle. Standard 1948-era toxicology testing found none of the
  usual suspects -- no cyanide, alkaloids, phenols, or barbiturates --
  despite investigators believing poisoning was the likely mechanism.
  [Grunge; True Crime Society]
- At the time, pathologist John Barkley Bennett and pharmacology
  professor Cedric Stanton Hicks both suggested a rare, precisely timed
  poison such as digitalis or the toxin ouabain/strophanthin could kill
  without leaving detectable traces by the methods available in 1948.
  In 1994, John Harber Phillips -- then Chief Justice of Victoria and
  chair of the Victorian Institute of Forensic Medicine -- reviewed the
  case and concluded digitalis was, in his view, the likely cause.
  [strangeoutdoors.com; Wikipedia] This remains a retrospective medical
  opinion, not a proven finding -- no poison was ever actually detected
  in his body.
- In May 2021, the body was formally exhumed by South Australia Police
  (Operations Persevere/Persist, an initiative targeting the state's
  historic unidentified remains) specifically to obtain more reliable
  DNA than what had previously been extracted from hair embedded in an
  old plaster death mask of the man. [ABC News: exhumation]
- In July 2022, University of Adelaide professor Derek Abbott, working
  with genetic genealogist Colleen Fitzpatrick, announced they had
  identified the man via DNA and a genealogical family tree of more
  than 4,000 people: Carl "Charles" Webb, an electrical engineer born
  in Melbourne in 1905. South Australia Police later formally confirmed
  the identification. [CNN; Smithsonian Magazine]
- Even with his identity resolved, the cause of his death, why he
  traveled to Adelaide, the meaning of the code, and any connection to
  Jestyn all remain unresolved. [CNN; Wikipedia]

## Theories, attributed

- **Rare/undetectable poisoning (Bennett and Hicks, at the time;
  retrospectively endorsed by Justice John Harber Phillips, 1994)**:
  digitalis or a similar cardiac-glycoside poison, in a precisely timed
  dose. *For*: fits the absence of external injury and the negative
  results on standard-of-the-time toxicology. *Against*: it's an
  inference built by ruling out other causes and matching symptoms,
  not a positive detection -- no poison was ever actually found in his
  body.
- **Cold War-era espionage (a theory popularized by journalists and
  authors over decades, tied to the era's tensions and Adelaide's
  proximity to the Woomera rocket range)**: the missing identity, the
  removed labels, the code, and unusually fine/imported tailoring on
  some of his clothes have all been read as signs of a man who didn't
  want to be traced. *For*: the pattern of deliberate anonymity really
  is unusual for an ordinary death. *Against*: no declassified
  document or intelligence service has ever named him or confirmed any
  connection -- it remains atmospheric, not evidentiary.
- **A personal connection to Jestyn (raised by researchers following
  the phone-number link, and by Jestyn's own daughter's later public
  statements)**: not a full cause-of-death theory so much as an
  identity/motive thread. *For*: the phone number and matching Rubaiyat
  edition are a real, undisputed physical link, and the daughter's
  on-record account is a real family statement. *Against*: Jestyn
  denied it to police throughout her life and died without ever
  confirming what, if anything, she actually knew.

Nobody living is accused of anything here -- this isn't even confirmed
to be a homicide -- so the "no naming a living private individual as a
perpetrator" rule isn't a live concern, but Jestyn's daughter's account
should stay clearly attributed to her, not stated as established fact.

# Research brief: The Isdal Woman (1970)

Case check: yes. A full timeline, real forensic detail (including a
genuine 2016-17 reinvestigation with modern isotope analysis), and at
least two genuinely distinct threads (official suicide ruling vs.
espionage speculation) -- enough for a real 15-25 minute episode without
padding. Low-violence, atmosphere-first, no graphic content required to
tell it -- a good fit for the calm "Vanishing Hours" tone.

## Established facts

- On 29/11/1970, a man and his two young daughters hiking in Isdalen
  ("Ice Valley"), a remote wooded valley outside Bergen, Norway, found
  the body of an unidentified woman. [Wikipedia: Isdal Woman; A&E]
- The front of her body -- face, most of her hair, torso, and limbs --
  was severely burned, her facial features destroyed beyond
  recognition; the back of her body showed comparatively little damage.
  [AllThatsInteresting; Grokipedia: Isdal Woman]
- Autopsy found soot in her lungs, meaning she was still alive when the
  fire started. Cause of death was recorded as a combination of carbon
  monoxide poisoning and barbiturate overdose: she had taken an
  estimated 50-70 Fenemal-brand sleeping pills (a barbiturate). Her
  blood concentration at time of death was roughly half what it takes
  to lose consciousness -- not lethal on its own at that exact moment,
  but the full ingested dose would eventually have been fatal even
  without the fire, once fully absorbed. [Wikipedia; Medium/The Fig
  Files case summary]
- All identifying labels had been cut out of her clothing. [Wikipedia;
  Life in Norway]
- Two suitcases matching her were found at the Bergen train station,
  containing wigs, non-prescription glasses, prescriptions with their
  labels removed, clothes with labels cut off, and foreign currency
  from Germany, Norway, Belgium, Switzerland, and the UK.
  [AllThatsInteresting]
- Investigators traced her movements through Norway from March to
  November 1970: she had stayed at hotels across the country under at
  least six different aliases, including "Genevieve Lancier," "Claudia
  Tielt," "Claudia Nielsen," "Alexia Zarne-Merchez," "Vera Jarle," and
  "Elisabeth Leenhouwfr." Eight fake passports under different
  identities were recovered. [Wikipedia; Unidentified Wiki]
- She paid entirely in cash, extensively, despite no traceable source
  of income. Her final known movements include buying a train ticket
  from Stavanger to Basel, Switzerland on 01/04/1970, and staying at
  two different hotels in Paris in late October 1970, about a month
  before her death. [NZZ reporting]
- Norwegian police officially classified her death as suicide, a
  conclusion that has remained disputed and controversial ever since,
  given the elaborate false identities and disguised travel that don't
  obviously fit the profile of an anonymous suicide. [Wikipedia]
- In 2016, Norwegian journalist Marit Higraff and an NRK/BBC
  investigative team ("Death in Ice Valley") reopened the case with
  modern forensic tools, including stable-isotope analysis performed on
  her teeth (preserved from the original 1970 autopsy). The results
  suggested she likely spent early childhood in the Nuremberg region of
  Germany, then moved during adolescence toward central/eastern Europe
  and later France, possibly around the WWII period. [Mental Floss;
  multiple case summaries citing the NRK/BBC investigation]
- DNA testing has been performed on her remains in recent years but has
  not produced a database match to any known missing person. [A&E]

## Theories, attributed

- **Suicide (official Norwegian police ruling, 1970, never formally
  revised)**: she took a large dose of barbiturates intending to die,
  and the fire (self-started or otherwise) finished it. *For*: matches
  the physical/toxicological evidence directly -- the barbiturate dose
  alone would eventually have been fatal. *Against*: doesn't explain
  why an anonymous suicide would maintain eight false identities,
  disguise wigs, and cash reserves in five currencies, or why her
  clothing labels were deliberately removed both before and seemingly
  around the time of death.
- **Cold War espionage (widely circulated theory, developed at length
  by the NRK/BBC "Death in Ice Valley" investigation and decades of
  Norwegian press coverage)**: her use of multiple false identities,
  forged passports, extensive cash-funded travel with no visible income,
  and the disguises all read as tradecraft rather than an ordinary
  private life; Bergen itself held real strategic significance during
  the Cold War. *For*: the pattern of behavior genuinely resembles
  intelligence tradecraft more than anything else documented about her.
  *Against*: no government has ever officially acknowledged any
  connection to her, and more than 50 years on, nothing has moved this
  past circumstantial pattern-matching into confirmed fact.
- **A specific link to Swiss banker François Genoud (raised by an
  independent online researcher, more recent, reported by the NZZ)**:
  travel-log timing shows the Isdal Woman and Genoud -- a real,
  well-documented financier of far-right and extremist causes -- were
  both in Paris on overlapping dates in mid-1970. *For*: it's a real,
  documented overlap in two independently kept sets of travel records.
  *Against*: even the researchers raising it describe it as a weak,
  speculative coincidence with no documented direct contact between the
  two -- included here as a real recent lead, not as a likely answer.

No credible investigator has ever proposed a named living individual as
responsible for her death; this isn't a live concern for the script,
but the theories above still need the same hedged, attributed language
used elsewhere (see docs/episode-workflow.md).
